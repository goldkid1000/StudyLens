      The 360° protractor

      Drag the copper strike line around the center dot. On an azimuth protractor, 0° sits at North and the numbers climb clockwise. The three boxes below follow the needle — drag it and watch them change. They describe the needle, not any numbers you type into the calculator.

      Needle at000°
      Other end of the line180°
      Said as a bearingN0°E

      On paper, you would lay the protractor's center hole on your dot, keep its 0° aimed at the map's North arrow, and read the rim where your line crosses it.

      Protractor style:

      Real tool draws it like the clear plastic 360° protractor: an outer ring counting clockwise from North, plus an inner ring shifted half a turn — the inner ring reads the other end of your line, so both ends check at once. Your choice is remembered on this device.

      How to measure on paper — with a round protractor

      1. Put the protractor's center hole right on the dot.
      2. Line the 360/0 mark up with the map's north arrow — not the top of the page.
      3. Find your strike number on the outer ring and make a small mark there.
      4. Mark the number straight across too — the other end of the line (strike + 180°).
      5. Connect the two marks with a straight line through the dot — that's your strike line.
      6. Turn the paper so the strike line points up.
      7. Draw the short dip tick on the right, straight across from the strike line (perpendicular to it).
      8. Turn the paper back and write the dip number next to the strike line.
